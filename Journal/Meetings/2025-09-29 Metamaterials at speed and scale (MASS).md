## Purpose

- To discuss the "Metamaterials at Speed and Scale" (MASS) concept and a potential EPSRC bid around AI/ML-driven co-design of new materials and their manufacturing processes.

## Key Discussion Points

- Introductions.
- [[Andy Bell]] overview: getting better-quality data back into engineering design has been discussed a lot over the last 12 months, and a focus on metamaterials has come out of this. There is an EPSRC call for critical materials / UK sovereign capability, aiming to make better use of these - possible November deadline.
  - Framing the problem: new materials typically take over a decade to go from lab to market.
  - Proposed solution: an AI/ML process to enable seamless knowledge transfer between material design and material manufacturing - demonstrating the design of a new material in parallel with its mass-production system, shortening time to market by several years.
  - Initial thoughts: electrical machines as the focus area; magnets as a potential target, to use less rare-earth material.
- General discussion on materials between [[Lloyd Tinkler|Lloyd T]] and [[Simon Pope]].
- Proposed approach: start with 3 disparate strands - machine design, material design, and data - which gradually converge based on the data and processing. 3D printing seen as a key enabler.
- [[JS]] raised a "chicken and egg" problem: you need a production system to generate data, to allow optimisation, to then develop a production system. Could a model of a production system be used to generate synthetic data instead?
  - [[Lloyd Tinkler|Lloyd T]] noted this project is potentially coming up with both a new material and a new process simultaneously.
  - [[Andy Bell]] suggested using existing production machines already in place to handle the rudimentary work.
- Scale of project: no limit from EPSRC on value or time. [[Andy Bell]] initially thinking around £10m for a big project, of which roughly £2m might go towards building the machine itself (uncertain).
- Preference for an "action research" approach - do it, then work out why it didn't work, rather than spending lots of time on literature reviews.

## Decisions

- Preference agreed for an action-research approach over extensive literature review work.
