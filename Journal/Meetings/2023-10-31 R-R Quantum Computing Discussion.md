## Key Discussion Points

> [!note] What was talked about?
> Capture the substance - not a transcript, but the important points, context, and nuance that you'd want to recall later.

- [[HB]] set the scene: interest from Boeing, how to get ready for QC when it arrives, and links to COMPASS. What's the interest from R-R, and how can AMRC support?
- R-R are doing prep work for the arrival of QC but will not be investing in hardware yet.
  - Focus on skills and knowledge development for how to use the hardware.
  - Not IBM - concerns over IP agreements where IBM owns everything done on their QCaaS.
  - Aim to become an intelligent end user and applier of QC.
  - CFD, FEA, and Systems Engineering flagged as application cases.
  - Taking existing problems/use cases that are very hard to solve with current methods and getting them ready for QC.
  - MISSY data has been discussed as relevant.
- Advice: don't invest in anything yet (hardware, financially).
- NVidia emulators on Azure are getting better.
- Horizon scanning and risk mitigation activities are worthwhile; identifying use cases is key.
- Data quality, quantity, format, ontologies etc. - all unknown how these will influence Quantum approaches.
- Qubits flagged as a personal learning gap for [[JS]].
- Material microstructures flagged as a potential area of interest.
- Manufacturing distributions (e.g. outliers) flagged as a key use case.
- PhDs discussion - possibility of Tech Board sponsorship, and challenge of finding quantum expertise to supervise.
- Threats: encryption (hybrid classical/quantum risk to things like bank accounts), and small data packets/complex datasets.
- R-R working with Strathclyde on Quantum (ahead of the game - Prof [[Andrew Daley]], now at Oxford) and Bristol on AI (lead academic talking to chip designers).
- R-R currently has only 2 people working on Quantum across the UK - a small investment.
- There is a research "hub" involving Oxford, Strathclyde, and a couple of others.
- Composites manufacture flagged as a good use case.
- Exascale computing at Edinburgh, and Isambard-AI at Bristol (NCC campus), noted as relevant infrastructure.
