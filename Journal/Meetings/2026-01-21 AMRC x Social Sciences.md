## Key Discussion Points

- Round-table introductions covering AMRC and UoS SSH departments, broad research interests, and motivations for the meeting; agenda ran through past/upcoming collaborations, how the two capabilities fit into each other's world, the funding landscape, specific opportunities, and next steps.
- There's often a misunderstanding of SSH's role in bids - SSH is frequently pulled in last minute, largely because EPSRC has only recently started requiring it. Existing SSH collaborations include the Management School, Decision Science, and Supply Chain.
- [[SH-B]] gave an overview of SSH-related AMRC work, including Cymru (human robotics).
- [[Sharan]] raised in connection with Cymru and HVMC.
- [[Susan Oman]] - data will "make a comeback": the Bolster project is looking at data skills, and there's a vindication of data systems over data viz; trust is best built by working with people that groups already trust.
- [[Louise Dore]] outlined Management School-adjacent groups: the Institute of Work Psychology, the Centre for Decent Work, Social Psychologists, and "Place"/Urban Studies work - with an emphasis on where a new development is having an impact, linking to HVMC.
- [[Susan Oman]] distinguished a "**Trustworthy System**" from getting people to trust an existing one - Social Sciences is more concerned with the former (designing for trustworthiness) than engineering trust in what already exists.
- **Interdisciplinary funding** raised as a theme, with a mention of the Lindsay FLF route (uncertain/exploratory).
- [[Susan Oman]] noted interdisciplinary work is structurally difficult at UoS, partly because peer review isn't well set up to handle interdisciplinary bids.
- Successful interdisciplinary bids cited as reference points: TAS, Grantham, and Health Sciences. [[SH-B]] suggested learning from Health Sciences' approach to interdisciplinary projects; [[Susan Oman]] proposed a "training" exchange of examples of what worked and what didn't.
- [[Louise Dore]] is part of a central UoS function designed to support strategic priorities, indicating some top-down institutional support is available.
- Shared enthusiasm in the room to collaborate, tempered by [[Susan Oman]]'s observation that it's hard to get actual decision-makers to attend workshops.
- [[Louise Dore]] suggested seed funding as an incentive to get people to attend a session, alongside lighter-weight exploratory work such as joint site visits (not framed as a formal research output, more likely a blog post).
