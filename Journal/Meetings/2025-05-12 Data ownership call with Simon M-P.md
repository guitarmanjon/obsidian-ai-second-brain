## Key Discussion Points

> [!note] What was talked about?
> Capture the substance - not a transcript, but the important points, context, and nuance that you'd want to recall later.

- Legally there are two types of data: personal data (name, address, medical history etc.) and IP Rights.
- The standard AMRC T&Cs (section 8) include provision for IPR - for commercial work, AMRC does not own the foreground IP; it's owned by the customer.
- However, standard T&Cs are used in only a minority of projects in [[Simon M-P]]'s experience - many customers ask for custom agreements.
- JS explained the goal he was hoping to achieve, using Data Connect as an example.
- [[Simon M-P]] outlined two routes to get access to data: a license from the customer, or the data being vested in AMRC (AMRC owns it).
- Licensing is probably lower risk, since the data owner remains responsible for legal issues arising later.
- [[Simon M-P]] is willing to look into and work on anything needed, as he is paid to act on AMRC's behalf.
- Section 8.2 of the standard terms might cover what's needed, but many commercial agreements exclude it.
- JS asked whether it's worth having specific statements about data - [[Simon M-P]] could see the value in this.
- [[Simon M-P]] encouraged starting a conversation with Matt F, Angus, and Tom Brayford.
