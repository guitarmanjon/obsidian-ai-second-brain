## Key Discussion Points

- Aim is to develop capability in the machining group to increase lower-TRL funding routes (e.g. EPSRC).
- Push for more collaborations with academia, including international collaborations.
- Funding diversification can help mitigate the risk of reduced funding from Catapult/Board in future, and gives more flexibility in resourcing - up to twice as much engineering time achievable with EPSRC money vs IUK.
- Need to improve reputation in the funding body arena, and experience as Principal Investigators.
- Catapult has 4 years left, so ideally AMRC will have £1m turnover from EPSRC by then.
- Academics with strong EPSRC reputations are good collaboration targets.
- EPSRC options that can be submitted at any time: New Investigator, Fellowships, Responsive Mode.
- Publication guidance:
	- Conferences are only for new researchers or specific business development purposes; attendance must be agreed with line manager. European funding usually calls for conference attendance; other funding routes should focus on journal papers. If conferences are required, attendance should be strategic.
	- Journal papers should target high-ranking journals only.
	- All public dissemination (conference presentations, papers, journal papers) must be reviewed by the Head of Research ([[TMc]]) and then the Research Director ([[Keith Ridgway]]) before release.
- Need to improve grant writing skills - in development, with training opportunities at university to be used.
- Publication register:
	- Any planned publication must be entered on the register.
	- A short list of AMRC-targeted journals will be agreed.
	- Publication count is to be increased for all researchers; a list of technical staff and priority areas will be issued, and staff should be included in publications that fall in their area, engaging throughout to add value.
	- Strategic authors approach agreed if needed - certain people's publication record increased deliberately to help with grant proposals etc.
