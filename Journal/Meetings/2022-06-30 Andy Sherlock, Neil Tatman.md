## Key Discussion Points

> [!note] What was talked about?
> Capture the substance - not a transcript, but the important points, context, and nuance that you'd want to recall later.

- DS course/delivery at NMIS: data skills for workforce development, worth 10 Masters credits, run over 10 weeks at 3 hours per week, aimed at those with a DS background.
- Aims to upskill manufacturing engineers in DS basics with a roadmap for where to go next, and also to help data scientists better understand PLM, MES, etc.
- Delivered by Edinburgh, with [[Andy Sherlock]] contracted to teach on it.
- 20 people through the course so far in its first year; 80 additional funded places planned over 22/23, plus 34 NMIS staff interested.
- Delivery sandwiches DS topics and manufacturing topics on a week-by-week basis; data carpentry features heavily in individual projects based on real-world challenges from the cohort's companies.
- [[Neil Tatman]] raised a good point on IT risk around allowing data scientists to access (and download from) any database they need.
- [[Andy Sherlock]] has links to Anaconda.
- [[Neil Tatman]] raised the importance of "genealogy of data" and history - how was it captured, was it the same sensor as before, etc.
- [[Neil Tatman]] also raised implementing data capture and analysis safely - cyber and quality risks, lifecycle management, longevity ("not just for Christmas").
