## Purpose

- Cross-organisation meeting (AMRC, MTT, UoH, HVMC) to align on the origins, current status, and "what next" for the Full Monty project — technology, marketing, and funding — ahead of Mach 2020.

## Key Discussion Points

The attendee block and an agenda-topic list were interleaved in the source (source text garbled — column order unclear in the original export); the agenda fragments noted alongside attendees were: PM&C, Non-geometric validation, Splunk, ABG project, Amido. The rest of the page reads cleanly.

- Origins and rationale — [[Peter Drever]] (PD) wanted to get together to understand the origins of the project and where it's got to, and agree a plan for using, advancing, and getting the technology out there. [[Pete Willoughby]] (PW) explained where the idea came from, with two scenarios: demonstrating to the manufacturing community that legacy 5-axis machines don't need to be scrapped (retrofit/rebuild isn't cost-effective for 3-axis machines; refurb costs 50-75% of a new machine, with roughly half the delivery time; green impact is significant — castings for a machine of that size are energy-equivalent to powering ~200 homes); and [[Sam Turner]] (ST) suggesting sensors be added for servitisation, though servitisation itself has a limited sensor suite — hence wanting as many sensors as possible (originally 240, whittled down to a sensible number), tied to performance, availability and quality. Full Monty gives a fully-loaded machine to work with.
- ST's framing — a showpiece for the Catapult (digital tech, sensors, analytics), improving uptime/performance, applying data analysis and ML to machining with big data sets, and servitisation.
- PW's framing — caution about a "tech trap" where all the tech is fitted but not actually used; question of how to get engagement; agreement not to focus on tech in this particular session.
- Metrology hub tie-in — link with Versaken and Moschos at ACSE.
- Digital thread — PW noted 12 weeks of effort on reverse engineering to get the digital model; MMPS should be straightforward to take the model through — question of how to fit this into the ABG project. Mach 2020 digital thread: design → MMPS → data from machining → measurement data. Machine data and cutting parameters feed MMPS.
- Made Smarter bid — ST proposed a bid covering machine health, servitisation, refurb; PW agreed, suggesting collaborative research with an industrial partner such as RR or BAE, to whet appetite with initial projects.
- Augmented/virtual reality — ST sees AR/VR as helping market the machine; JS to talk to IMG about how quickly an AR model could be put together, or VR — possibly a headset demo at Mach 2020, with data streaming.
- Marketing — PW interested in Marketing's view; GC proposed a launch at Mach 2020, considering SMEs (servitisation could fit here), pricing as part of the story, "twice as good for half the price," and the green impact (no spend on castings/fabrication) as a big marketing angle. Size of machines can limit purchase of new ones, which favours refurb.
- Analogy — refurbishing a machine tool compared to buying an old house (value is in the land/structure, not necessarily the contents, which can be refurbed); PW compared it to leased cars, repurposed at end of lease.
- Small 3-axis machines remain a financial challenge.
- Servitisation — not yet prevalent in the machine tool market (though Scania and Volvo do it for trucks, John Deere for agricultural equipment); expected to pick up. ST, as part of the TES strategy team, noted big names (BAE, RR) are very interested in this model / flexible manufacturing.
- Marketing next steps — target Mach 2020; VR essential; at minimum show a video and some data, live link if possible. Katia flagged the need to build a story leading up to Mach and decide what to release and when to build interest.
- Further tech work — ST to target Made Smarter in April; start building a consortium now; tech heads need to start planning.
- MTA — GC asked whether anything has been done with the MTA; not yet, though the MTA itself would be supportive (members might show some resistance); [[James Selka]] will be supportive.
- Mike Lewis / IMG — [[Mike Lewis]] has budget for Maintenance 4.0 work, which could expand to look at machine tools; JS to speak to Mike, with ST potentially able to help.
- Next meeting — arranged for week commencing 25 November (26th AM), covering the marketing plan and tech-side input on next steps.
- Monetising — PW flagged this as important, including cost modelling.

## Decisions

- Group agreed not to focus on the technology itself in this session, prioritising engagement and marketing questions instead.
- Follow-up meeting agreed for week commencing 25 November (26th AM) to cover the marketing plan and next steps on the tech side.
