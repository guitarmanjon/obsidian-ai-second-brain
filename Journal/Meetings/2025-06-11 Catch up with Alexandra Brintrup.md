## Purpose

- General catch up between AMRC and the Alan Turing Institute to explore collaboration opportunities.

## Key Discussion Points

- Turing has reorganised into challenge areas; [[Alexandra Brintrup]] (AB) is now part of the Sustainability challenge area, so any collaborative work will need to focus on sustainability.
- Supply chain identified as a strong hook - [[Victor Shi|VS]] noted SMR (Small Modular Reactors) as a good sustainability hook that feeds into supply chain work. Big focus on supply chain generally, driven by AB's and Victor's interests.
- [[RS]] noted SMR supply chain overlaps heavily with defence (AUKUS), offshore wind, and hydrogen - steel tubes, pumps, pipes. Government interest in sovereign capability.
- Mapping exercise proposed as part of the collaboration - [[Victor Shi|VS]] suggested AI input for mapping companies and supply chain data, plus simulation techniques for disruptions etc; [[RS]] noted policy makers could be involved further down the line.
- Potential funding routes for a bigger call with multiple work packages: IUK, ESRC, EPSRC etc.
- Risk reduction angle raised - possible interest from Lloyd's Register.
- Spending review has promised £2bn for the AI action plan, plus significant funding for renewables and energy - plan to put together a 2-pager to start socialising ideas.
- Idea of identifying single points of failure in supply chains.
- MOU discussed, covering possible areas: Defence, Optimisation (process/supply chain/data), and Secure Data Sharing.
- Hackathon idea floated: a network provided to attendees, who present a solution to finding critical links in a supply chain.

## Decisions

- Any AMRC-Turing collaboration will focus on sustainability and supply chain, given AB's role in Turing's Sustainability challenge area.
