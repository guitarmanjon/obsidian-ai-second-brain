## Key Discussion Points

- ADViCE described as a strong partnership example ([[Alan Turing Institute]] + Energy Systems Catapult), funded through DESNZ.
- Keynote from [[Paul Monks]]: ongoing challenge of identifying how [[AI]] can maximise decarbonisation; [[AI]] driving clean energy growth.
- Project outputs presented: an "[[AI]] for Decarbonisation" report; white papers from the Expert Working Group; 7 grand challenges identified (unlocking domestic decarbonisation; enabling net zero infrastructure; maximising flexibility in the energy network; decarbonisation of manufacturing inputs; improving manufacturing process efficiency; optimising soil management; minimising methane in agriculture); [[Data Science]] Sharing Circles; the ADViCE Knowledge Base; blog posts including "Role of [[AI]] in Decarbonising Manufacturing."
- Panel on [[AI]] opportunities in decarbonisation: [[Lucy Yu]] highlighted forecasting of energy networks for supply/demand optimisation, and weather forecasting for generation impact (e.g. DeepMind's Gencast). [[Chris Baker]] noted Rothamsted has extensive data useful for validating [[AI]] models, stressing importance of validation/verification. National Data Library mentioned repeatedly re: building accessible, validated datasets. Centre for Net Zero has developed the "Faraday" model - a generative [[AI]] model (based on GMMs, not an [[LLM]]).
- Data access discussion: [[Thomas Baird]] noted many datasets are available to local authorities but are under-utilised because authorities either don't know about them or don't budget for access costs (e.g. Ordnance Survey, satellite imagery). [[Chris Baker]] discussed "data assimilation" - combining datasets depending on availability, e.g. farm field monitoring switching between ground sensors and satellite data if one fails.
- Model size discussion: example of TensorFlow Lite running on a Raspberry Pi - large models (e.g. [[LLM|LLMs]]) aren't always necessary.
- UK leadership in decarbonisation: [[Lucy Yu]] noted government R&D funding is key, citing past seed research leading to significant economic return; government also plays a convening role across academia/industry; investment needed across R&D, Skills, and Infrastructure (skills and infrastructure particularly important).
- Q&A - JS asked the first question, on balancing [[AI]]-for-decarbonisation efforts against the carbon cost of data centres used to develop [[AI]] models; panel agreed it was a strong, deep question.
  - [[Lucy Yu]]: DeepSeek showed models don't need to be as energy-hungry as previously thought; the [[AI]] Energy Council is examining future energy requirements, though these remain uncertain; potential efficiency gains from hardware/software optimisation (e.g. latest NVIDIA GPUs ~25% more efficient; algorithm optimisation also helps); downstream benefits of [[AI]] may outweigh current carbon cost; alternative non-[[AI]] work might be similarly carbon-intensive anyway.
  - [[Chris Baker]]: knowledge is power - new knowledge leads to better methods; algorithm optimisation is key.
- Question on choosing the right [[AI]] model for a task (not just [[LLM|LLMs]]): [[Lucy Yu]] noted the Faraday model, while generative, is not an [[LLM]] (based on GMMs).
- Question on regulation: focus needs to be broader than [[AI]] alone - quality datasets matter too, including adherence to FAIR principles.
- A project idea occurred to JS off the back of this session, around energy forecasting/decarbonisation - noted separately in his project ideas notebook.
