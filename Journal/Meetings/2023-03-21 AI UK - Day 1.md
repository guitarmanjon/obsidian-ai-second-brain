## Purpose

- Attend Day 1 of the AI UK conference, covering sessions on Trusted Research Environments, foundation models, and public perceptions of AI.

## Key Discussion Points

- **Workshop: Trusted Research Environments for Sensitive Data** ([[Hari Sood]] and [[David Sarmiento Perez]])
  - All data destroyed at the end of a project - question of what happens if long-term access/use is wanted.
  - Azure-based; the Turing TRE aligns with government and community reports; open source.
  - Available software is restricted to a standard toolset - additional software can be added if approved, though how approval works and who decides was unclear.
  - Question raised: is the Linux-based remote desktop environment the only option? What about off-the-shelf solutions such as Databricks?
  - Compliant with the NHS Data Security Protection Toolkit.
  - TRE people were based on the top floor of the AI UK venue.
- **"Are foundation models the right foundation for AI?"**
  - [[Mhairi]]: concerns around the drivers for foundation models (commercial, corporate) and accessibility of models.
  - [[Marcus]]: developing terms for proving trust, reliability and accuracy hasn't been great over the last 18 months; we won't get the bedrock needed for future AI systems from current foundation models; need world models as a foundation; common-sense knowledge in AI is not there and is a long way off.
  - Little belief in the room that AGI will be solved by current development in what is essentially prompt completion (e.g. Bard, ChatGPT).
  - Quote attributed to Jan LeCun(?): LLMs are "an off-ramp on the way to AGI".
  - Vectors making a comeback; SVMs have largely disappeared from public eye as few people used them.
  - Book mention: "Rebooting AI" by Gary (Marcus).
  - Prompt engineering discussed.
  - [[Mhairi]]: AI as a decision-support tool for humans, rather than a human replicant - we always have a choice in how we use AI.
  - [[Maria Mulholland|Maria]]: scary how easily the tech can be used incorrectly - foundation models are not the solution but a tool to develop solutions.
  - [[Michael]]: danger of people relying on the outputs as sage advice.
  - When LLMs get it wrong or make things up, it's called a "hallucination"; "factuality" refers to investigating how truth can be embedded.
- **Public Perceptions of AI** (host [[Anjana Ahuja]]; [[David Leslie]] remote; [[Tracey Brown]] - Sense about Science; [[Tania Duarte]] - We and AI; [[Ben Morrow|Ben]] - DSIT)
  - Pugh research: 15% of the US public are more concerned than excited about AI.
  - [[Tracey Brown]]: big issue is that the public doesn't know who is making decisions on when and where AI will be used - where does the power lie?
  - [[Tania Duarte]]: positive experiences of AI will improve public perception and trust; the question people should be asking is "what are you being sold?"; need increased diversity among people working in and developing AI.
  - [[David Leslie]]: do AI researchers care about what the public think?
  - [[Ben Morrow|Ben]]: likens AI use to electricity - it can be used for great things but also harm, so the application and use of AI will determine public perception.
  - Trustworthiness: [[Tracey Brown]] - people need to be involved in the ultimate decision about their actions; who is responsible? (There's a risk the public thinks responsibility isn't theirs once an AI system is involved, but everyone needs to take responsibility for how these systems are used.)
  - [[Tania Duarte]]: behind all AI are people making decisions on training data and models - there's a human process behind all AI tools. AI literacy isn't included in AI standards but is important.
  - Some discussion of including AI in the National Curriculum; mention of an "AI ombudsman"; reference to a Frameworks survey from April 2022.
