## Purpose

- Attend Day 2 of the [[AI]] UK conference, covering the [[Alan Turing Institute|Turing]] Strategy Launch, a Finance and Responsible [[AI]] panel, and a "Can [[AI]] Save our Planet?" panel.

## Key Discussion Points

- **[[Alan Turing Institute|Turing]] Strategy Launch** ([[Anjana Ahuja]] hosting; [[Doug Gurr]]; [[Adrian Smith]])
  - [[Adrian Smith]]: 3 focus points - defence and security, health, and environment (biodiversity and climate); mentioned the [[Alan Turing Institute|Turing]] university network.
  - [[Doug Gurr]]: chair of the board of trustees; 3 key points - focus, additionality, impact.
  - A Parliamentary undersecretary for [[AI]] was in the audience.
- **Finance and Responsible [[AI]]** (L-R: [[Henrike]], [[Lukasz]], [[Marta]], [[Ray]] - host, [[Charmaine]]; also Paul)
  - [[Marta]] has worked in automated verification.
  - Intro by [[Marta]]: responsible automation has been around for a long time, with [[AI]] following up next; [[AI]] to date has been founded on performance rather than safety and explainability. Where does responsibility lie for failure? E.g. a self-driving car getting a speed limit wrong - was it the [[AI]] algorithm, the developer, or the driver?
  - [[Henrike]]: organisational change is needed for [[AI]] uptake and development.
  - Paul: model risk management for [[AI]] adoption; fairness is a challenge (traditional credit models are perhaps not so fair); question of whether it's ethical to use other data sources to augment customer data for decisions like loans.
  - [[Lukasz]]: mentioned Project FAIR, a [[Alan Turing Institute|Turing]] project.
  - [[Charmaine]]: Environment and Social Governance - the finance sector has a huge responsibility here.
  - Question on additional risk introduced by tools like ChatGPT: [[Charmaine]] - human-in-the-loop needed to validate model outputs; risks include data leakage and accuracy. [[Marta]] - human feedback to the model means bias is inevitable; optimisation is limited (is it statistically significant?); answers are based on text search/models, not reasoning. [[Charmaine]] - IP risk: who owns the resultant IP from an interaction with ChatGPT? Paul - sceptical about ChatGPT but can see how it could improve customer service.
  - Question raised (by JS) about application to other industries: Paul - process is key; [[Henrike]] - governance; [[Lukasz]] - trust.
- **Can [[AI]] Save our Planet?** (L-R: [[Beatriz]], [[Aidan]], [[Maria Mulholland|Maria]], [[Alastair]], [[Natasha Lethbridge|Natasha]])
  - Compute tax discussed.
  - [[Aidan]]: use of simpler models capable of tackling many tasks, also improving efficiency - the human brain remains unmatched here; his spinout uses a stochastic process in cement manufacture - could this be applied elsewhere in manufacturing?
  - [[Natasha Lethbridge|Natasha]]: saving the planet will be a systems challenge.
  - Real-world problems topics covered: brain-computer interfaces; probabilistic [[Machine Learning|ML]]; using [[AI]] to mark student science exam answers (comparing ChatGPT, BioBERT, and Long T5).
  - Biodiversity and [[AI]]: [[Hannah]] - crowdsourcing for image analysis and tagging; citizen science and crowdsourcing are a big part of obtaining ground truth in biodiversity studies. Question raised: can manufacturing do the same, using people on the ground to give ground-truth data?
