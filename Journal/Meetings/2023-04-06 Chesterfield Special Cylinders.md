## Key Discussion Points

> [!note] What was talked about?
> Capture the substance - not a transcript, but the important points, context, and nuance that you'd want to recall later.

- Bid in progress, deadline 26th April.
  - James Harris from Element2 suggested AMRC storage to 700-1000 bar.
  - Novelty lies in bringing together all the technologies around monitoring and sensing for hydrogen storage.
  - 10% of the cost in hydrogen is in storage.
  - At its heart, this is condition monitoring - [[Frank Ashton]] is familiar with condition monitoring from his previous role at Cummins (refuse truck CM).
  - Cylinder design specifies a number of cycles of use; condition monitoring moves towards a tailored "storage as a service" model (predictive maintenance).
  - No existing data known to them; might be able to get some from known sources, but not necessarily related to hydrogen.
- InnovateUK bid details:
  - Max £1m per bid.
  - Competition: hydrogen storage and distribution supply chain collaborative R&D (UKRI).
  - Other participants: High Energy, Element2, possibly off-the-shelf sensors (Endress), Luxfer (cylinders).
  - Up to 18 months; start by 1st December 2023, end 31st May 2025.
  - No trailers currently being built in the UK for hydrogen transport.
  - [[Ian Williamson]] and [[Frank Ashton]] to start writing the bid in the coming days - defining work packages, who takes the majority of the work, and where funding is coming from.
  - AMRC's contribution: data flows, data science, data engineering - question of what can be shared to help Frank understand this.
  - [[Frank Ashton]] believes every cylinder needs to be sensed; [[Rab Scott]] raised traceability of each cylinder via a "digital passport".
- Outline of AMRC's approach: Sensors -> Data capture -> Edge analytics/feature extraction -> Data transport -> Data architecture -> Data science & analytics -> Visualisation -> Action.
