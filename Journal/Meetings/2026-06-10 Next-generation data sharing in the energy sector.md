## Attendees

- [[Jon Stammers]] (attendee). Hosted by [[Josh D'Addario]]. Panellists: [[Dave Simpson]] (DNV), [[Rachel Hassall]] (DNV), [[Michael Glass]] (SSEN Distribution), [[Jon Thompson]] (Yottar), [[Yiu-Shing Pang]] (UK Power Networks), [[Abbas Mahmood]] (Energy Networks Association).

## Purpose

- Jon attended a talk/panel event on next-generation data sharing in the energy sector.

## Key Discussion Points

- Hosted by [[Josh D'Addario]]. [[Dave Simpson]] from DNV - DNV help companies in the energy sector make the most of their data. Example of a good public energy sector dataset - the London Register of Subsurface CO2 Storage. ISO 25012 quoted - "data quality characteristics will be of varying importance and priority to different stakeholders."
- Panel: [[Rachel Hassall]] (DNV, [[Data Science]] and [[AI]] lead), [[Michael Glass]] (Data Governance and Information Manager, SSEN Distribution), [[Jon Thompson]] (Chief Product Officer, Yottar), [[Yiu-Shing Pang]] (Data sharing manager, UK Power Networks), [[Abbas Mahmood]] (Head of Data and Digitalisation, Energy Networks Association).
- Challenges and opportunities: [[Rachel Hassall]] noted the opportunity for e.g. Kaggle competitions to derive real value from open datasets and find new insights in energy sector data - also used for training new models and foundational [[AI]]. [[Jon Thompson]] - open data is really useful for startups; if data isn't adequately labelled, it can be more of a burden than a bonus. Trust in data - both in the dataset itself, and in how it is used (and abused).
- Hurdles to improving collecting, sharing and using open data: [[Michael Glass]] (MG) - metadata challenge is significant; Ofgem data best practice principles; referenced the ENA data triage playbook; data described/defined differently across organisations - data standards, schemas, ontologies, interoperability of data. [[Yiu-Shing Pang]] (YP) - DNOs have different ways of describing things, so standardisation is a major hurdle. [[Jon Thompson]] (JT) - need to balance the benefits of publishing open data vs. the risks of doing so, e.g. critical infrastructure data being used by harmful actors.
- Rules for industry, government, society, academia to help the energy sector get to the next level: YP - referenced Ofgem's consultation on securing open data / triage data best practice guidance; risks of sharing data can reveal locations of infrastructure - often the reason given for not sharing data, but the location of infrastructure is usually obvious anyway. MG - stats on who has downloaded data from the portals and how they use it helps with guiding next steps.
- Current work helping push forward next-gen data sharing: already happening - governance, development of standards etc.; data consistency; sharing lessons learnt, scaling good examples. Future - collaboration across government, ODI, DNV etc. to move this forward.
- Q&A: JS's own question - are there any examples of good data sharing in the energy sector where there has needed to be some degree of desensitising data, due to IP or commercial restrictions? How was the challenge approached with the data creator/owner? YP - a use case relating to data centres, commercially sensitive - used utilisation percentages for data centre data, not the location, zero identification of the data centre in question, with legal team input. JT - a really useful dataset for a startup in the sector; what happens behind the meter was really useful. Q around responsibility for the commercial model for infrastructure behind cooperative data sharing, given the benefits of sharing aren't always as great for the sharer as for the user - MG gave an example of a collaboration where people take responsibility for their own costs, and referenced open banking, where this is a two-way street with benefits back to the data sharer - a change of culture needed for people to understand the benefits. [[Rachel Hassall]] (RH) - energy system as a whole, benefits can permeate throughout from data sharing. Q around EVs and better data sharing off the back of EV takeup / autonomous driving - vehicle-to-grid data useful to DNOs, so trust and governance are key. How prolific is FAIR data in the energy sector? JT - there's progress but still gaps - findable is seen, but interoperability is a challenge.
- Resources shared: ODI explainer on data institutions; Ofgem consultation on securing open data / triage best practice guidance; ENA data triage playbook; NESO Virtual Energy System data sharing infrastructure page; UK Power Networks data centre demand profiles dataset; Energy Networks Association website.
