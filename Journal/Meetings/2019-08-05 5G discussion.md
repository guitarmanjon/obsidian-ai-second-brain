## Purpose

> Cross-centre AMRC discussion to decide how to collectively approach an open 5G funding call - who leads, main bid themes and ownership, and potential partners.

## Key Discussion Points

> [!note] What was talked about?
> Capture the substance - not a transcript, but the important points, context, and nuance that you'd want to recall later. The page contains both raw live notes and a cleaner "Summary for email" recap of the same meeting; the recap is used below as the primary account, with anything uniquely captured in the raw notes added afterward.

**Summary (from the "Summary for email" recap)**
- BAE expressed interest in leading a bid with AMRC, and Siva had confirmation that they will lead. However, BAE want AMRC to do the legwork, and don't want to host any hardware (happy for it to be hosted at AMRC NW).
- BAE keen for Manchester University to be involved for AI expertise, but caution is needed around too many academic institutions given the 30% funding rule (limited cash to go around).
- BAE were involved in previous 5G work (possibly phase 1 Innovation Network) - Siva to find out details, and also to investigate what BAE actually want from involvement, as this wasn't entirely clear.
- Andrew reminded the group that Colin has stated there will be a cross-AMRC project, codename "i-Net", linking all AMRC centres.
- Ryan summarised his view: AMRC can be a strong 5G testbed candidate, with ambitions bigger than Erdem's original proposal (though that work should be part of it). Each centre has a use case to put 5G to the test - e.g. F2050 pushing bandwidth limits with the Smart Factory demonstrator, FoF looking at latency for real-time machining monitoring/control, NW potentially looking at security.
- General discussion of technical applications: VR/AR, battery monitoring, real-time machining, 5G-enabled sensors/monitoring devices, additive and subtractive machining, robotic arms (5G bypassing complex cabling), a future-proof open-source-friendly framework for manufacturing (building trust in 5G, dispelling myths), remote edge devices sending data to centralised HPC with control signals sent back, autonomous factory vehicles offloading processing to centralised compute, and public vs private (local) 5G networks.
- Recurring key question: why is 5G needed over cable/WiFi for each proposed application? Clear for some cases (EV battery monitoring, Richard's rail/Ricardo example), less so for others - needs clarifying for every technical application AMRC wants to pursue.
- AMRC should target 5G applications in areas it's already expert in (machining, digital manufacturing) rather than developing new expertise from scratch.
- Bid practicalities: Kay relayed feedback from a London briefing that applicants should build on/extend existing work (UK 5G Innovation Network phase one) rather than duplicate it; the catapult will coordinate cross-project delivery and share best practice.
- SME involvement is essential - suggestions included looking at BAE's supplier list, MTT, and cyber security firm IOETEC.
- Charlotte flagged that the review process considers the whole portfolio of submissions, so bids too similar to another proposal risk only one being funded.
- Need more industrial collaborators to satisfy the 30% funding rule. Siemens have shown interest (based on Erdem's proposal) - what could they bring? BAE have also mentioned Vodafone.

**Additional detail from the raw notes (not carried into the recap)**
- More granular, speaker-attributed back-and-forth is present in the raw notes (individual contributions from Siva, Richard, Omer, Kay, and Charlotte on specific technical questions), largely consistent with the summary above but with more attribution detail.
- Raw notes specifically flag Siemens' interest via Sinumerik Edge / MindSphere connectivity, with a caution about what Siemens would want in return and whether AMRC would just be testing their kit; the group decided they were happy to use Siemens' kit, with Siemens supporting rather than leading.
- A closing thought recorded only in the raw notes: "Human in the loop - get better info to the human decision maker involved" and reliance on HPC for the majority of analysis.

## Decisions

> [!warning] What was agreed?
> Record any decisions made, who made them, and any conditions or caveats. Decisions are the highest-value output of most meetings.

- BAE will lead the 5G bid, with AMRC doing the legwork; hardware to be hosted at AMRC NW rather than at BAE.
- AMRC should focus its 5G proposal on application areas where it already has expertise (machining, digital manufacturing) rather than developing new expertise.
- Siemens to be involved in a supporting capacity only, not leading.
