## Key Discussion Points

> [!note] What was talked about?
> Capture the substance - not a transcript, but the important points, context, and nuance that you'd want to recall later.

- Intro to SAS — [[Lorcan Matthews]] (pre-sales), [[Harry Clark]] (academic relations manager). SAS is a central platform applied across all industries, including edge analytics and sensor data; rationalises data to a central location; data visualisation is also a big part of the business. Full pipeline: data aggregation → analytics → visualisation. Founder/CEO is Dr James Goodnight.
- SAS IoT Analytics — [[Tim Clark]] (Global IoT Commercial Lead). Internal + external data brings value to analytics ("how does this device perform in its ecosystem?"). Framed as "edge to enterprise" — SAS does analytics, not asset connectivity. Well aware of servitisation of machine tools (possible Full Monty application). Mentioned Neonjay camera, from Adilink (sp?).
- Results as a service — SAS has its own base language plus solutions and services.
- Framing: need to understand the business problem and its cost before looking at a cost-appropriate solution. "Power of 1" — what would a 1% saving per day mean, e.g. 1% less downtime?
- SAS platform overview — analytics lifecycle: Data → Discovery → Decision (repeat); SAS Challenge Champion; SAS platform for data (Access, Integrate, Cleanse, Govern), for discovery (Report, explore, forecast, predict, optimise, govern), and for deployment (Govern, Embed, Monitor, Automate). Real-time (near) noted as a genuine need in manufacturing. Two short fragments also appear alongside this section that don't clearly connect to the rest — "ESP - event stream processing" and "OCF ??" — included here for completeness (source text garbled — column order unclear in the original export).
- Enterprise analytics for education — TUoS licence available.
- AML overview — [[Roger Singleton]]. AML is a 2008 spin-out doing consultancy, prototyping and production. Pattonair is pushing for their SME supply chain to be more digitally enabled, driving AML's current interest in SAS (AML in a good position with Pattonair). Similar trend seen elsewhere in manufacturing, e.g. JLR's digitally-enabled supply chain; IBM TradeLens as a blockchain-based through-life product example.
- AML runs all Mori Seikis — mill-turns (NT) and vertical mills (NMV). CMM data currently printed off and shipped with the part; question raised about linking machine tool measurements and CMM data, and CMM fluctuations alongside tool change data. IT provider described as "Everything Is Cloud."
- AML's digital roadmap has three strands: (1) operational — OEE, machine connectivity; (2) CAD/CAM design, methods, knowledge share — focus of Armada; (3) supply chain connectivity, e.g. knowing when parts have shipped so tooling can be prepped.
- Catapult funding — possible AMRC Catapult-funded project looking at SMEs and embedding analytics; challenge is data availability in SMEs. [[Roger Singleton]] has previously done analytics looking at individual operation time contribution to total cycle time.
- [[Tim Clark]] raised gamification of data dashboards.
- Innovate UK Business Basics 3 flagged as a possible funding option.
