## Purpose

- Kick-off session between [[MTC]] and AMRC for the [[Rolls-Royce|R-R]] Factory of the Future (FoF) project, under the ADAPT programme.

## Key Discussion Points

- [[Matt Chamberlain]] and [[Alison Gamble]] gave an overview of what they were hoping to achieve with this project. Comes under the ADAPT programme - "Advanced Disassembly, Assembly & Performance Testing". Timeline for the new facility for Entry into Service (EIS) is 2035. Target is 32 engines per week, but 10 years out there will be MRO operations on top of this (3x, 4x the production volumes) - AG: focus on assembly for this project. So far nothing has been designed and nothing sold in terms of a new engine, but moving from a 22hr (current) to 4.5hr takt time is key. The 10 technology areas shared in the SoR have been further broken down into 2-3 sub-topics, shown in the slide pack.
- **Design**: opportunity to feed into engine design to facilitate assembly - design for automation, design for assembly - could include artefacts on the products that facilitate automation (e.g. grasping points for a robot handler). Changes to legislation in the coming 5-10 years may affect design philosophy - e.g. [[Airbus]] removed the requirement for people to go inside fuel storage in wings during assembly.
- **Jointing**: bringing together cylindrical parts that need to be balanced. How can this be automated? Can parts be matched for assembly based on earlier inspection (e.g. Pringle shapes matching up accordingly)? Digital thread/digital passport? Potential for new fastenings, one-way assembly?
- **Inspection**: currently assemblies get full inspection through the assembly process - human activity at present. Future desire is to be fully digitised, 100% in-line inspection (not a separate activity) - eliminate manual visual inspection activities. AG - "Digital twin of every product we make"; [[Jamie McGourlay]] (JMc) - careful, need to define what's meant by [[Digital Twin]] (of what - assembly, operation, machining, etc.).
- **Engine balance & runout**: key question is how this can be done differently in future to avoid all Return-to-Shop (RTS) due to imbalancing. Sometimes imbalancing is due to inconsistencies in the supply chain, requiring remeasure - links back to the Inspection work; interoperability of measurement data could help reduce the need for remeasuring.
- **Bonding**: remove all wet assembly methods - i.e. using silicone-like sealants to fill gaps where there shouldn't be gaps, usually in hard-to-access places, to eliminate air ingress (sealants applied similarly to bathroom silicone). Could this be automated, if needed? Clean & seal? Sealant setting time can also be considered (e.g. faster cure via automated application, UV curing, sealant viscosity more suited to automated application). Wet assembly involves taking assemblies apart, cleaning them, and re-assembling.
- **Tooling**: should be "digitised at the point of use", standardised across the product range - at present tools aren't specified, fitters use whatever tool they see fit → lots of variation. Calibration tracking is a must, easier with standardised tooling. Covers "big tools" (structures to hold engines, need lots of space) all the way down to hand tools.
- **Instructions**: current practice is "paper on glass" - docs aren't printed, just on a screen instead. Ideal future is no written instructions, fitters interact with instructions. Automated creation of instructions - currently very manual, can this be automated? (Claude for instructions? Capturing tacit knowledge?) Two sets of instructions - assembly and MRO. Standardisation and QA are key features. H&S now included in instructions - will this change in future? Question of whether final inspection can be minimised/avoided if assembly is verified against instructions on the go.
- **Connectivity**: well covered in the last project - "tell us more" (AG). What could be the make-up of the data being dealt with? Where can the data go - cloud vs. on-prem? JMc - cloud will be part of the equation, data storage infrastructure *will* change. [[Rolls-Royce|R-R]] data policies on data management and where the data can go. Who will be defining the data requirements, and when? Currently being defined - AG has a tech work programme on Digital, [[Leon Oswin]] leading this area now.
- **Shop environment**: H&S is key. [[Rolls-Royce|R-R]] have some guiding principles to be shared in the slide deck. Digital Twins of the factory, viewed through different lenses - whole flow, down to workbench, down to ergonomics. Floor-based carry and lift system defined in a previous project - a great proposal. Clean floor is ideal - no fixturing of anything to the floor, but not at the cost of factory performance. An assembly line that could just go into a warehouse, without any serious structural work needed, is ideal (e.g. buying an old Amazon warehouse and bringing in assembly).
- **Test**: 100% RFT, minimal test needed, shorter engine tests with standardised rigs. Proposal around the equipment/process that supports the test, such as rigging and derigging. The test itself is a separate piece of work. Could there be a floor-based rig that carries the assembly throughout, and lifts into the test harness? Note that the size of the test bed cannot change due to the volume of air required, but getting the engine in/out could be automated.

**General key points**
- Assembly vs. MRO - could consider how sacrificing cost in assembly leads to cheaper MRO, so lower costs in the long run (other assembly-MRO tradeoffs can also exist).
- Don't be surprised if some [[Rolls-Royce|R-R]] people think the proposals are way off (based on their current working practices and knowledge).
- There will be a range of engine products within one family, due to airframers' requirements (variants of an airframe, e.g. 737 MAX 8, MAX 9).
- Fitters will need to reskill/deskill with the introduction of automated technologies - the demographic/make-up of the future workforce will need to be different. Culture is not being tackled directly anywhere, but will be the biggest blocker (JS's opinion).
- Design - a "blank sheet" opportunity: design not started/fixed yet, so anything can be suggested for input into the design - but that means the work is almost limitless. Design will be very focused on engine performance at this stage (e.g. must be 10% better than current to even get a seat at the table for discussion), perhaps without much consideration for how to make it. Rough design at present: narrower core, gearbox at the front, roughly business-jet-sized core, fan the size of a Trent 700 - AG suggested taking a look at the GE90 engine.
- Priority order at present: **Performance → Cost → Rate**.
- Engine externals (pipes, cables etc.) will become more challenging due to no longer being on the external casing - they'll be on the core, pipes stacked maybe 3 layers deep. Very manual process to fit clips to pipes.
- Logistics - not a key consideration but there will be thought (at [[Rolls-Royce|R-R]]?) into requirements from the supply chain on pre-packaged assemblies, how these are packaged to minimise unpacking time. [[Tom Robinson]] (TR) mentioned the BMW motorcycle plant in Germany with automated logistics inside the factory.
- Guiding principles will be shared, including things like "no night shifts". Culture is key.
- Toyota Production System came up - note that it didn't eliminate people in the factory, it just changed their role.

- For the skills bit - define the roles of the future first, then consider the skills needed.
- [[MTC]] will create an Excel workbook for everyone to work from.
- Won't split the 10 items because both centres will have contributions into all of them.
