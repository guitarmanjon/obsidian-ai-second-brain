## Summary

- Session: "Making Data Work for You", presented by Louise Butcher, Principal Data Scientist at Hartree Centre / STFC.
- Date: 07 January 2025, 13:32.

## Key Takeaways

- **Why is data important?** It can **add value & improve efficiency**, drive **better decisions**, help **understand risks**, allow **more effective control** of products, and supports **regulation/reputation** needs (e.g. GDPR).
- **What can data science do for a business?** Three kinds of questions:
  - **Descriptive** — what's happening?
  - **Predictive** — what's going to happen?
  - **Prescriptive** — how to change what will happen.
- **Why is *good* data important?** Example given: the **Google Photos "gorillas" mislabelling incident** — people misclassified as gorillas — as a case of **demographic bias** baked into training data.
- **Data gathering pitfall — survivorship bias**: data that only reflects "survived" examples can skew conclusions.
- **What constitutes "good" data** (from a pasted slide screenshot): data must exist and be accessible; you must have the right to use it (open licence, client ownership, or an agreement); personal data requires subject consent or another lawful basis; and it should be free from bias, sufficient in volume, collected consistently, clearly defined (what it measures, its units), labelled where required, and have low error rates.
- **UK GDPR basics** (from a pasted slide screenshot): adopted from EU law into UK law; governs how personal data is used, stored and shared; sets out principles for handling data and the legal bases under which it can be processed (e.g. consent).
- **Data management policy / curation lifecycle** (from a pasted diagram screenshot): data moves through **creation → collection → processing → analysis** (grouped as "data curation"), then **preservation** (maintain, keep up to date, redact), **re-use** (is it still useful?), and **deletion** (does it expire? is it irrelevant? should it legally be removed?).
- **Case studies** presented (each as a pasted slide screenshot with minimal accompanying notes — topics only, not transcribed in detail):
  - **Automated data integration** — Roberts Vain Wilshaw (chartered surveyors): Hartree built an automated pipeline combining varying open/business data sources into an integrated database to speed up lead generation.
  - **Forecasting energy use** — South West Water: ML/time-series model (Python/R) to forecast energy use per waste-water treatment plant and enable fair site-to-site comparisons.
  - **Smart crop protection** — with IBM and Rothamsted Research: pest-risk prediction models aimed at helping farmers plan preventative measures, reduce crop losses, and lower insurance costs.
  - **Predicting care pathways** — NHS Liverpool Central Commissioning Group: ML model predicting which post-hospital interventions (community, social, mental health) a patient would need after discharge.
