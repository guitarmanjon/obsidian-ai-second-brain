- **Parliament**
  - Scope for impact into parliament: Legislation & Scrutiny, Members & Peers, All Party Groups, Resources.
  - Resources: UK Parliament website + Hansard; Parliamentary Office for Science & Technology (**POST**) → available to all, not just MPs; Knowledge Exchange Unit; Libraries (Commons + Lords) → Briefings.

- **Legislation**
  - Government Bills dominate life in parliament.
    - Institute for Government (.org.uk) lists all bills for this parliament that were in the King's Speech.
  - Private Members' Bills introduced by backbenchers.
    - Backbenchers often don't have teams to support them.
    - Draft Bills are work in progress and can accept evidence to the committee → **opportunity for impact**.

- **Scrutiny** — two types: Questions and Select Committees.
  - **Questions**
    - Oral + Written.
    - Oral — usually a bland question followed up by a supplementary question that "sticks the needle in."
    - Written — MPs can ask any number.
      - Must relate to information held by the department, or actions & decisions of ministers.
      - Questions & answers can be found via the Parliament website.
      - "All questions considered?" (noted as a question in the source)
      - **ABC for answers**: accurate, brief & clear.
  - **Select Committee** (called "Scrutiny Committee" in Scotland & Wales)
    - Inquiries; hold MPs (departments) to account.
    - Report produced at the end.
    - Government must respond within **8 weeks** (not days).
    - Committee members are backbenchers, cross-party, working together.
    - Researchers & academics can contribute: give evidence, invite committee to visit university, act as an advisor.
    - All evidence is on the record.
    - Science, Innovation & Technology committee probably most relevant; maybe Business & Trade too.
    - Find Select Committees on the parliament website — current inquiries shown; a green tick means currently open for evidence; sign up to weekly alerts.
    - If submitting written evidence, include the line: **"I'd be happy to give oral evidence if needed."**

- **Support from MPs**
  - Understand their world.
  - Keep briefings to **2 pages** (not a full report): 1-sentence summary, killer facts, "what do you want me to do?"
  - MP toolkit — ways an MP can act for you: 1. Correspondence, 2. Meetings, 3. Tabling questions or motions, 4. Raise on the floor of the House, 5. Seek publicity.

- **All Party Groups (APPGs)**
  - Informal cross-party groups with **no official status** within parliament.
  - Not just MPs + Lords.

- **House of Lords**
  - Focused on revising legislation, rather than party politics.
  - Permanent committees, plus temporary "Special Inquiry" committees.

- **Government**
  - Scope for researchers: liaise with key departments (know your minister; roles & responsibilities); Chief Scientific Advisors.
  - Policy tools for research work: white papers, policy docs.
  - Government policy = anything a Minister says to Parliament.
  - **Civil servants**
    - Civil servants advise, ministers decide.
    - Advice is rational and based on facts, in accordance with the Government's commitments.
    - Typically a 2-page briefing with 3–4 options is put to the minister, with one recommendation.
    - Difficult to find out which civil servant is relevant to your work.
    - Frustration is to be expected when engaging civil servants (both ways).
    - Chief Scientific Advisors have staff who read papers, attend conferences, etc.

- **Consultations**
  - Can submit evidence into consultations.
  - Consultations influence policy & regulations.

- **Routes for Academic Engagement**
  - Dame Angela McLean (noted as **Chief Scientific Advisor**) is proactive in calling for academics to engage.
  - Civil Service is starting to look at having specialists, not generalists.
