## Key Takeaways

> [!tip] The essential insights from this resource
> Distill the most important points. What would you want to remember if you came back to this in 6 months? Use progressive summarization, bold the critical phrases.

- **Track record statements** — reviewing example proposals:
  - Look at the Track Record sections of the proposals in the Example Proposals Folder.
  - Identify what claims and evidence/or factually verifiable statements are made.
    - What is the point of these statements?
    - What buttons are they trying to press?
    - Of what are they trying to convince the reader?
  - [Re-expressing the same issue here.]
  - What were particularly convincing parts? Can you use similar devices?
  - Did the team element come across well? If so, why?
  - What organisational suitability arguments are made?
- **NI statements — Exercise 1: SUBMITTED NI STATEMENTS**:
  - Pick several examples of NI statements from the National Importance Folder.
  - Read through and critically appraise them.
  - What arguments were made for their proposal's importance? What were the areas targetted? (Academia, commerce, industry, government, society etc.)
  - What references to evidence were made? (Gov policy document, horizon scanning, ...)
  - Were the arguments specific or general (i.e. that work in a general area was important)?
  - Were arguments qualitative or quantitative?
  - Were you convinced?
  - Can you use anything yourself from what you have read?
