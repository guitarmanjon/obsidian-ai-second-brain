## Summary

- Handwritten training notes from a **Project Fundamentals Qualification** course, dated **09/05/2023**, covering portfolio/programme/project structures (P3M3), the difference between strategic ("doing the right things") and delivery ("doing things right") focus, tools for analysing environmental factors, and a topic list for upcoming course content.

## Key Takeaways

- **P3M3** relates to selection, oversight, and review of all change initiatives.
- Hierarchical structure noted: **Portfolio** sits above multiple **Programmes**, each Programme contains multiple **Projects**, and each Project contains multiple **Work** packages.
- **Mid-to-long term transformational changes** vs **short-term tactical changes**, alongside ongoing **business as usual**.
- Two guiding questions for portfolio/programme management: **"Are we doing the right things?"** and **"Are we doing things right?"**
- Portfolio-level functions/disciplines listed: **Management Control, Benefits Management, Financial Management, Risk Management, Stakeholder Management, Organisational Governance, Resource Management**.
- **Understanding environmental factors** — tools noted:
  - **VUCA**: Volatile, Uncertain, Complex, Ambiguous.
  - **SWOT**: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats.
  - **PESTLE**: Political, Economic, Sociological, Technological, Legislative, Environmental.
- Page 88 includes a pasted photo of a printed reference page (numbered "105") showing a matching exercise linking **Quality Planning, Quality Assurance, Quality Control, and Continuous Improvement** to their definitions (inspection/testing vs acceptance criteria vs standard processes vs process improvement from past experience). The connecting lines in the source image cross over each other, so the exact pairing of each term to its definition is not reliably legible from the note and was not transcribed in detail.
- List of **"Topics to go over"** noted for the course: Benefits Management, Change control process, Belbin, Deployment baselines for linear + iterative (approaches), Success Criteria, Risk Management process, Time boxes.
