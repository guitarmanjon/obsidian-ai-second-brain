## Key Takeaways

- Use the latest [[Windchill]] slide template; be cautious with laser pointers (exaggerates nervous shaking; invisible on multi-screen/web setups — PowerPoint's built-in pointer/cursor is visible to all).
- Reference all sources; make images/graphs as large as possible (one slide per graph if clearer); talk to the audience, not the screen.
- Use a timer rather than relying on the host; aim for high-contrast/greyscale images since projector contrast is often worse than a laptop screen.
- Avoid large amounts of text (audience won't read it, and you'll be tempted to read it aloud); prefer images/diagrams over descriptions.
- Have a clear story arc: Introduction → Hypothesis → Method → Results → Observations and Conclusions.
- Volume: no need to shout — open your mouth wider (rule of thumb: enough to fit two fingers between your front teeth).
- Animations: behaviour varies across PowerPoint versions — test on the presentation computer beforehand; know your click points.
- Videos: avoid formats needing special codecs — .wmv/.mp4 are safe.
- Slide control: arrow keys (forward/back) and mouse wheel work, not just clicking.
